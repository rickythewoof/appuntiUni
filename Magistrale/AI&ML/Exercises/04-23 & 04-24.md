# Exam Exercise
Regression exercise from exam of June 10, 2024:
COnsider sequence of actions
$\rho = move(B); move(C); move(D)$. check whether $\rho$ is executable in $s_0$.
This is an executability (or legality) problem!

To do this, we need to check whether:
1. $move(B)$ is executable in s_0, ie $D \models Poss(move(B), s_0)$
2. $D \models Poss(move(C), do(move(B), s_0)); s_1 = do(move(B), s_0)$
3.  $D \models Poss(move(D), do(move(C), do(move(B), s_0))); s_2 = do(move(C, s_1)$

To check the first formula, we can see that we are just wondering about the initial situation. The regression of that is itself.  $R[poss(move(B), s_0)] = \exists y.R[AgentAt(y,s_0) \wedge Right(y,B)] = \exists y. AgentAt(y,s_0) \wedge Right(y,B)$

Regression theorem tells us that, for that formula:
$$
D_{s_0} \cup D_{una} \models \exists y. AgentAt(y,s_0) \wedge Right(y,B)
$$
which is true for y = A.

Let's check the second formula ; $D \models Poss(move(C), s_1)$
The regression is $R[Poss(move(C), do(move(B), s_0))]$ 
We take the righthand side of the Action Precondition Axiom and we get $\exists y. 
- $R[AgentAt(y, do(move(B),s_0))]\wedge Right(y, C)$
We'll regress even further, by  taking the SSA, instantiating on y and that situation, and replacing it with the righthand side:
- $AgentAt(x, do(a,s_0)) \leftrightarrow a = move(y) \vee AgentAt(y, s) \wedge \neg ((\exists z. a = move(z) \wedge z \neq y) \vee a = exit())$
	- where $x = y$, $a = move(B)$, and $s = s_0$
- $move(B) = move(y) \vee AgentAt(y, s_0) \wedge \neg ((\exists z. move(B) = move(z) \wedge z \neq y) \vee move(B) = exit())$
- $\exists y. R[AgentAt(y, do(move(B),s_0))]\wedge Right(y, C)$
